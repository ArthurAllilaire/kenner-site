Kenner vs Aquant: AI for field technicians compared

Aquant is an enterprise AI platform for servicing physical products: a voice agent technicians can call, vision AI through the phone camera, customer self-service and call-center agents, and analytics for service leaders, aimed at equipment makers and large service organisations. Kenner (getkenner.com) is narrower on purpose: a personal assistant for HVAC/R service teams that lives in the messaging apps technicians already use, answers from a knowledge graph of your own units and writes the paperwork back to your FSM. Pick Aquant if you are an equipment maker, or run support across call center, self-service and field. Pick Kenner if you run commercial HVAC or refrigeration service and want technicians helped on real jobs within a week.

Arthur Allilaire, CTO at Kenner10 November 2026 · Updated 13 November 2026 · 5 min read

In short

  • Aquant is the broader platform: field, call center, customer self-service and analytics, for OEMs and large service organisations across many equipment industries.
  • Kenner is the focused one: built for HVAC/R service teams, reached in the messaging apps technicians already use, live in a week.
  • They overlap more than most pairs on this blog. Both can be reached by phone, both draw on service history and manuals, and both write the job up.

What Aquant does well

Voice and vision together

On its field service page, Aquant describes a technician calling its voice AI, then turning on the phone camera so vision AI can read the equipment in real time, identifying make, model, condition and fault indicators. Guidance is then spoken step by step, synced with service history, manuals and the company’s own expertise. It needs no headset or smart glasses, only the phone.

One platform across the whole service operation

Aquant is not only for technicians. Its platform page covers customer self-service by phone and video, call centers where the AI resolves the call and hands over to a person when needed, and an analytics product for service leaders with team scorecards, parts usage trends and benchmarks against peers. If you are an equipment maker supporting dealers, customers and your own engineers, that breadth matters.

Built for regulated, complex equipment

Aquant names medical devices, industrial equipment, manufacturing plants, food equipment, high-tech and energy among its industries. It says its outputs are auditable and that any recommendation can be traced back to its source. Its interfaces include offline access, which matters in plant rooms and basements.

Where Kenner is different

It lives in the technician’s own messaging apps

Kenner is a contact in WhatsApp, iMessage, Microsoft Teams or RCS, and a normal phone number. A technician sends a text, a photo or a voice note the way they would to a colleague, in the app they already have open all day.

It is built around HVAC and refrigeration

Kenner reads the systems a refrigeration service team actually has: store and rack controllers such as Danfoss Alsense, Copeland Site Supervisor, Emerson E2/E3 and CAREL boss, next to the FSM and the document store. After a leak repair or a top-up it asks for what refrigerant records need under rules such as EPA 608 and F-gas, writes that to the unit’s record and books the follow-up leak check.

A knowledge graph of your own units

Kenner links every unit to the jobs done on it, its alarms, its parts and its manual. Answers start from the unit in front of the technician and show where each part came from. We explain the approach in knowledge graph vs RAG for field service.

A small, fast start

You start with one team. In the first week we connect to your systems and five to ten technicians save Kenner as a contact. For two weeks it works real jobs, and at the end you see what changed in your own numbers. You work with the founders throughout.

Who should pick which

Pick Aquant if you make equipment or run a large, multi-channel service organisation, you want the same AI across customer self-service, the call center and the field, or live camera guidance is central to how you want to support technicians.

Pick Kenner if you keep commercial HVAC or refrigeration running, as a contractor or in-house, and you want technicians to get answers and finished reports through the messaging apps and phone they already use, starting this month with one team.

Either way, test on your own data. Our five questions for any vendor are a fair place to start, and they apply to us as much as to anyone. To see Kenner on your own jobs, read how a pilot runs.

Common questions

Is Aquant only for equipment manufacturers?

No. Aquant names OEMs and service providers, and describes products for field technicians, call centers, customer self-service and service leaders.

Does Kenner offer live camera guidance like Aquant’s vision AI?

Technicians can send Kenner photos and voice notes in the chat, and Kenner can send back a photo, such as the page of the manual that shows the part. Live video guidance of the kind Aquant describes is not something we claim.

Which is faster to start with?

Kenner goes live within a week: we connect to your systems, technicians save it as a contact, and the first briefs and reports run in the same week. Aquant says most teams see measurable improvements within weeks of go-live.

Do both work without a new app?

Yes. Aquant says technicians can simply call its voice assistant. Kenner is reached in WhatsApp, iMessage, Teams, RCS or by a normal phone call.

Which is better for HVAC and refrigeration?

Kenner is built for it: it reads store and rack controllers, collects what refrigerant records need and reaches technicians where they already message. Aquant serves many equipment industries, and its customer stories include Comfort Systems.
