Kenner vs the AI built into your FSM: Agentforce for Field Service and Dynamics 365 Copilot

Salesforce and Microsoft both ship AI inside their field service products. Agentforce for Field Service briefs mobile workers before a job, helps them troubleshoot and summarises the work. Copilot in Dynamics 365 Field Service summarises and updates work orders and searches product manuals. Both work inside the vendor’s own app and data. Kenner (getkenner.com) works on top of whichever FSM you run, reaches technicians in WhatsApp, iMessage, Teams or by phone, and joins the FSM to the systems around it. If one platform holds everything your technicians need, use its AI. If the answer is usually spread across several systems, add Kenner.

Arthur Allilaire, CTO at Kenner10 November 2026 · Updated 13 November 2026 · 6 min read

In short

  • Agentforce for Field Service and Copilot in Dynamics 365 Field Service are the natural first choice if your technicians already work in that vendor’s app and the data they need is in it.
  • Kenner works with both. It adds what sits outside the FSM, and it reaches technicians in the messaging apps they already use and by phone.
  • Kenner leaves scheduling and dispatch to your FSM. It does not replace it.

What Agentforce for Field Service does well

Salesforce’s pricing page describes it as assistive AI for dispatchers and mobile workers. For the technician it lists a Pre-Work Brief, On-site Knowledge Search, Multimodal Troubleshooting and a Post-Work Summary. For the office it lists schedule summaries, help resolving schedule gaps, an asset health score and capacity planning.

Salesforce has also thought about the drive to site. In its deep dive it describes technicians playing back the pre-work brief on their phone through a Siri shortcut, so they hear it without taking their eyes off the road.

If Salesforce is your system of record for work orders, assets and knowledge, the strength is obvious: one vendor, one data model and one set of permissions.

What Copilot in Dynamics 365 Field Service does well

Microsoft’s documentation lists what Copilot does today: work order summaries on demand, with status, priority, related activities and recommended next actions; work order updates from natural language, typed or spoken, in the mobile app, currently in preview; and questions in natural language about the data in the system. On its product page Microsoft adds that Copilot can search lengthy product manuals for answers.

For the office there is inspection template creation from PDFs or images, and an agent feed for supervising agent-generated tasks. And the price is hard to argue with: Microsoft’s pricing page lists Copilot assistance as included in the Field Service licence.

Where Kenner is different

It works across systems, not inside one

The AI in an FSM answers from that FSM. In commercial HVAC and refrigeration, half the story is often somewhere else. Kenner reads store and rack controllers (Danfoss Alsense, Copeland Site Supervisor, Emerson E2/E3, CAREL boss), manuals and old reports in SharePoint or Confluence, and parts and stock in the ERP. It links them to the unit in a knowledge graph, next to the work orders from your FSM. See knowledge graph vs RAG for field service.

It meets technicians where they already are

Technicians save Kenner as a contact and use WhatsApp, iMessage, Microsoft Teams, RCS or a normal phone call. Nobody has to open the FSM’s app to ask a question or file a report.

It is built for HVAC and refrigeration

After a leak repair or a top-up, Kenner asks for what refrigerant records need under rules such as EPA 608 and F-gas, writes it to the unit’s record in your FSM and books the follow-up leak check.

It writes back to your FSM

Kenner does not become another system of record. With Salesforce Field Service, the record goes to the work order and the asset. With Dynamics 365 Field Service, it is written back to the work order and the asset as well.

Who should pick which

Use the built-in AI if your technicians already work in the Salesforce or Dynamics 365 mobile app all day, the knowledge they need is maintained in that platform, and you want scheduling and dispatch help as much as help on the job.

Add Kenner if the answer to a fault usually depends on data outside the FSM, your technicians prefer to text or call, or you want reports and refrigerant records written from a voice note and saved to the work order.

The same reasoning applies to other platforms. If you run ServiceTitan, read Kenner vs ServiceTitan Atlas. For an independent enterprise platform, read Kenner vs Aquant.

Common questions

Does Kenner work with Salesforce Field Service?

Yes. Kenner reads work orders, assets and past visits from Salesforce Field Service, and the record goes back to the work order and the asset.

Does Kenner work with Dynamics 365 Field Service?

Yes. Kenner reads work orders, assets and bookings from Dynamics 365 Field Service and writes back to both the work order and the asset.

How much does Agentforce for Field Service cost?

Salesforce lists Agentforce for Field Service at $125 per user per month, and includes it in its Field Service Max edition at $650 per user per month. Prices were checked on Salesforce’s pricing pages on 10 November 2026; confirm with Salesforce which base licence you need.

Is Copilot included in Dynamics 365 Field Service?

Microsoft’s pricing page lists AI assistance from Copilot as included in the Dynamics 365 Field Service licence at $105 per user per month, paid yearly, and says agents require Copilot Credits.

If we already have Agentforce or Copilot, why add Kenner?

Add Kenner when the answer to a fault is not all in the FSM: alarms in the store controller, manuals and old reports in SharePoint, parts in the ERP. Kenner links those to the unit in a knowledge graph and reaches technicians by text or phone call. If everything they need is already in the FSM, the built-in AI may be enough.
